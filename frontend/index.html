    At a glance

    The four pages

    Admin — the control panel for organizers. Use it to create tournaments and lobbies, manage rounds and scores, and work with your team. TV View — a public page at /tv/<id> that shows live standings, results, and the bracket. Nobody can change data on this page. Show it on a large screen or send the link to all players. Registrations — a sign-up page. Players register themselves and answer your questions before the event starts. Player Hub — the personal dashboard of each player at /player. It shows all active and past events of the player under one passphrase.

    No accounts needed for players

    Players do not create a username or a password. The app gives each player a unique passphrase: a short sequence of words. The passphrase opens the personal match view, score entry, and the Player Hub. The organizer can give the passphrase as a printed card, as a QR code, or by email.

    Formats and tools

    The app has three tournament formats: Group + Play-off, Mexicano, and Direct Play-offs. It also has co-editors, score entry by players, announcements, and links that you can share. When you convert a registration lobby into a tournament, all player passphrases stay the same.

    Run your first tournament

    1) Create the event

    Open the Create tab. Select a format (Group + Play-off, Mexicano, or Direct Play-offs) and a sport. Enter the names of the players or teams and the names of the courts. For Mexicano, also enter the points per match. The scoring and pairing settings of each format are in the Advanced Settings section of the same form. Click Create Tournament.

    2) Give players their codes

    Open the Player Codes panel. Each player has a passphrase (for example, brave-little-tiger) and a QR code. You can print the QR cards, copy one code, or send all codes by email. A player needs only this code to see their matches and enter scores. Players do not need an account.

    3) Put it on a screen

    Open /tv/<id> on a large screen, or send the link to the players. You can also set a custom alias for this address. The page shows live standings, courts, and the bracket. Nobody has to log in to see it. In the TV Settings panel, select the sections that the page shows. You can also select if players can enter their own scores.

    4) Run the rounds

    Enter the scores when the matches finish. In Mexicano, confirm the proposed pairings of each round to go to the next round. Before you confirm, you can look at the alternative pairing sets. If the format allows it, you can add or remove players during the event.

    5) Finish

    When the group stage or the Mexicano rounds are complete, click Start Play-offs to seed the bracket. In this step, you can also add wildcards or late entries. When the tournament has a champion, export a self-contained summary as an HTML or PDF file. The summary contains the bracket and all match results.

    Where things live
    • Mexicano settings — Advanced Settings in the Create form. After you create the tournament: Admin → Settings.
    • Player passphrases and QR codes — the Player Codes panel.
    • Sections of the public view, and score entry by players — the TV Settings panel.

    Demo mode

    Full demo (shared admin login)

    The public demo site runs the full app. Log in with the shared admin account to use all features: registration lobbies, clubs, communities, the Player Hub, custom addresses, and more. All visitors share the same data. The site deletes this data at regular intervals. Do not use the demo site for real events.

    Throwaway demo account

    On the main site, the login dialog has the link “Open the demo sandbox”. This link opens a separate practice site, which immediately creates a personal demo-xxxxxx account for you. You do not have to sign up. The site generates a passphrase, which is the password of the account. Save the passphrase to log in again from this device or from a different device. On this device, the login dialog of the demo site shows your account name when you come back. A sandbox has a limited set of admin functions: one active tournament at a time, and no registration lobbies, clubs, communities, custom addresses, or Player Hub profiles. The app deletes the account and all its data a few days after you create it (3 days by default).

    Steps for players

    1) Join

    Players register through a lobby. A lobby is on a public list, or it needs a join code. Players answer the questions that the organizer wrote. At registration, each player gets a unique passphrase: a short sequence of words. This passphrase is the personal key of the player in all parts of the app.

    2) Open your personal area

    When the tournament starts, players enter their passphrase or scan their QR code. This opens their personal match view. The view shows only their own pairings, courts, standings, and score entry. The organizer can give the passphrase as a printed card, as a QR code, or by email.

    3) Play

    Players can see the live standings, their next pairings, and their courts. If the organizer enables it, players can also enter or confirm scores from their own view. This decreases the work of the organizer during the event.

    4) Get updates

    Announcements, match comments, and optional email updates give the same information to all players during the event.

    5) Player Hub: one passphrase for all events

    A player can create a Player Hub profile at /player with only an email address. The profile gives the player one permanent passphrase for all events. When the player registers for a lobby, the app fills in the name, email, and contact automatically. When the tournament starts, it shows on the Hub dashboard together with all other active and past events of the player.

    The Hub also shows career statistics: matches played, win rate, and best rank. You can also see your history with one specific player across all tournaments: the matches as partners, the matches as opponents, and your win rate in each case.

    A player without a Hub profile can still link past events. To link an event, enter the passphrase that you received for that event.

    Steps for organizers

    1) Prepare

    You can create a tournament directly with a fixed list of players. Or you can first open a registration lobby to collect players before the event. A lobby has these functions: custom questions (for example, skill level or availability), an optional join code that controls who can sign up, a public list of registered players, and co-editors who help you manage the registrations.

    2) Launch

    Convert a lobby into a tournament with one click. Select the format (Group + Play-off, Mexicano, or Direct Play-offs). Set the group sizes, the court names, and the scoring rules. The app adds all registered players automatically. Each player keeps the same passphrase, so codes that you printed or sent by email before the event stay valid.

    3) Send the player passphrases

    In the Player Codes panel, you can see, copy, download, or print all passphrases as QR-code cards. You can also send the passphrases by email, to one player or to all players. Players can link their passphrase to their Player Hub profile. Then they see all their events in one place.

    4) Run rounds

    Generate pairings, assign courts, and enter or edit scores during each round. For Mexicano, compare the proposed pairing sets and select the best balance before you confirm. If the format allows it, you can replace players during the event. When the group rounds finish, start the play-offs and seed the bracket. At this step, you can add wildcards or late entries.

    5) Work with your team

    Invite co-editors. Co-editors have full access to the event operations: pairings, scores, and announcements. Some actions are only for the owner and the platform admins: delete the tournament, manage player passphrases, and change the core settings.

    6) Communicate and finish

    Post announcements. They show on the view of each player and on the TV display. Send email notifications to all players or to selected players. When the event is complete, export a self-contained HTML or PDF summary with the full bracket and all match results. Then archive the tournament to keep your list short.

    Main features

    Registration lobbies

    Custom questions, review of registrations, co-editors, and conversion into a tournament with one click.

    Live operations

    Round management, score entry, match comments, announcements, and changes to the player list during the event (in formats that support it).

    Player access and self-scoring

    Each player logs in with a QR code or a passphrase. Then the player sees their own matches and can enter scores.

    Public TV View

    A live page that you can share. It shows standings, results, the bracket, and the other sections that you select. You can set how often the page refreshes.

    Communities and clubs

    Use communities as the base level. Add a club when you need branding, seasons, and more club tools.

    Communities set the scope

    Tournaments and registration lobbies belong to a community. A community sets the default assignment of new events. It keeps the events of each league or venue separate. It also sets the scope of the ELO ratings and rankings: global, community, or club.

    Clubs add identity and progression

    A club is built on one community. It adds a logo, player tiers, seasons, leaderboards, tools to assign tournaments, and co-editor tools for organizer teams that run regular events.

    When to stay at community level

    Use only communities if you run occasional events, short series, or simple sign-ups. You still get all tournament functions, lobby conversion, and the TV and public pages. You do not have to maintain a club player list.

    When to upgrade to a club

    Create a club when the same organizer team runs regular events. A club gives a permanent identity, control of the player list, co-editor tools, and continuity across seasons. Also use a club for tier-based progression and for club leaderboards.

    Scope changes and data integrity

    You can move tournaments and lobbies to a different community, for example to clean up or migrate data. If the new community does not match the current club or season, the app removes those assignments automatically. This keeps the scope data valid.

    Recommended setup pattern

    First, create communities to set the structure. Then change the active communities into clubs. Assign each event to the correct season. Keep the default community and the player lists up to date when you add more events.

    Checklist

    Set a default community for admins. Before you open registrations, make sure that each tournament has the correct club. Before each event starts, check its season. Use club branding only where a long-term identity is important.

    Tournament formats

    Group + Play-off

    The app puts the players into groups, and each group plays a round-robin. Then the best players or teams of each group go to a knockout bracket. This format supports padel scoring and tennis scoring.

    Team mode: fixed pairs play against all other pairs in their group (a full round-robin). Player (Americano) mode: partners change each round, so each player plays with every other player at least once. The app makes the matchups of each round from the current group standings, so that each round stays competitive.

    Mexicano + Play-offs

    The app generates the pairings of each round from the current standings. Leaders play against leaders, and lower-ranked players play against players of a similar rank. In each round, the organizer can compare several proposed pairing sets and select the best balance. The number of rounds can be unlimited (you stop when you want) or fixed.

    The Advanced Settings give detailed control of scoring and pairing:

    • Skill gap — the maximum rating difference in one group. If you leave it blank, the app uses snake-draft order.
    • Win bonus — extra points for the winner on the leaderboard. The app adds them to the game points.
    • Rival strength (0–1) — a bonus for playing against strong opponents, from their average score. A higher value gives a larger effect.
    • Loss discount (0–1) — a multiplier for the points of the losing team. Example: with 0.75, a 12–20 loss gives 9 points, not 12.
    • Balance tolerance — the difference in team strength that the pairing engine can accept to prevent repeated matchups.
    • Teammate / opponent repeat weights — penalties when a player has the same partner or opponent again. Higher values give more varied combinations.
    • Repeat decay — makes old repeats count less than recent repeats.
    • Partner balance weight — a penalty for partners with very different skill levels.
    Direct Play-offs

    Start directly with a bracket. Seed the players in the order that you want, and the app generates the bracket immediately. You do not need a group stage. Use this format for knockout events or for draws with fully custom seeding.

    Single and double elimination

    Single and double elimination are available in all formats with play-offs. Espejo is available in Group + Play-off and in Direct Play-offs, but not in Mexicano. Single elimination (knockout): a player or team that loses one match is out. Double elimination: a parallel losers’ bracket gives each player or team a second chance. A finalist must lose two times to be out, so consistency across the full draw is important. Espejo: a consolation bracket for the players who lost in round 1. The two brackets run separately. The champion of the winners’ bracket is the tournament champion. The winner of the consolation bracket is the consolation winner. The event finishes when both brackets are complete.

    ELO rating system

    An ELO engine measures the skill of each player across tournaments. The engine uses the score margin, not only the result. Ratings change after each completed match, and the Player Hub shows them.

    Blended outcome

    The actual score is not only a win or a loss. It combines a result value W with a margin ratio R. Thus a close win and a large win give different rating changes.

    R = 0.5 + (yours − opp) / (2 × (yours + opp)) S = α · W + (1 − α) · R

    yours = your points. opp = the points of your opponent. W = result: win, draw, or loss (1, 0.5, or 0). R = margin ratio. α = blend weight (0.5). The 0.5 in the R formula is a centering constant: a draw gives R = 0.5. This constant is not related to α.

    S is the blended actual score. It replaces the simple win/loss value in the classic ELO formula. A higher S means a better result. The ELO calculation compares S with the expected score. The difference sets how much your rating changes.

    Rating update

    After each match, the app compares your blended result S with your expected score E. If your result is better than expected, your rating goes up. If your result is worse than expected, your rating goes down.

    E = 1 / (1 + 10(opp_rating − your_rating) / 400) Δ = K × (S − E) new_rating = old_rating + Δ

    E = expected score (0–1), from the rating difference. Against a higher-rated opponent, your E is lower, so a win against a stronger player gives more points. When S > E, your rating goes up. When S < E, your rating goes down. K sets the maximum change per match (see K-factor tiers below).

    K-factor tiers

    Ratings of new players change faster: K = 40 for the first 20 matches, K = 20 up to 40 matches, and K = 10 after that. New players get to their correct level quickly, and the ratings of experienced players stay stable.

    Reliability dampener

    The rating of an opponent with very few matches is not reliable. When an established player (20 or more matches) plays such an opponent, a reliability factor decreases the rating change. This prevents large rating changes that come from provisional opponents. Provisional players always get the full rating change, so their ratings settle quickly.

    reliability(n) = 0.3 + 0.7 × n / 20   (capped at 1.0) Δadj = Δ × reliability(opponent_matches)

    n = number of completed matches of the opponent. The factor applies only to established players (20 or more matches). With 0 opponent matches, the change is 30% of the normal value. With 20 opponent matches, the change is 100%. In doubles, the app uses the lower reliability of the two opponents. Provisional players (fewer than 20 matches) always get the full rating change, so that their ratings calibrate quickly.

    Doubles (2v2) adjustment

    The expected score uses the average rating of each team. A partner-strength multiplier corrects for pairs with different ratings. If a strong player has a weaker partner, wins give more points and losses cost fewer points. The opposite applies to a weaker player with a stronger partner. The multiplier is between 0.5× and 1.5×.

    Minimum delta

    A winner always gets at least +1 ELO. A loser always loses at least −1 ELO. This rule applies for all margins and expected scores, so each match result changes the rating.

    Starting play-offs from group stage or Mexicano

    From Group + Play-off

    When all group matches are complete, the Start Play-offs button shows. Click it to get a recommended list of qualified players, in the order of the group standings. Select or clear any player to change the list. You can add external / seeded participants with a name and a score, for example wildcards or late entries that did not play the group stage. Select single elimination, double elimination, or Espejo, and confirm. The order of the selected players sets the bracket seeding.

    From Mexicano

    Play-offs are optional. You can start them after any Mexicano round. When you start them, a team builder opens. Each seed slot of the bracket has player drop-down lists, which the app fills in from the leaderboard ranking. In individual mode, select two players for each slot to make one bracket team. The app shows their combined leaderboard score to help you. In team mode, each slot contains one existing team. You can replace any slot with an external participant. When all slots are correct, select single elimination, double elimination, or Espejo, and start the bracket.

    External and late-entry participants

    In both formats, you can add participants who did not play the qualifying stage. Put them directly into any seed slot of the bracket and give them a score manually. Use this for seeded wildcards, guests, or late registrations.
